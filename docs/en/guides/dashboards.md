# Dashboards

A **dashboard** is one screen that answers "how are we doing?" without anybody having to open five things and add up in their head.

It's a canvas of tiles — charts, single big numbers, timelines — each reading from your own data. Open tasks by status. Progress against a deadline. What's on this week. A counter's current value. Whatever the person who asks the question actually needs to see.

![An initiative dashboard](../images/tools/dashboard.png)

## Dashboards only display

Nothing on a dashboard can be edited from it. Not a design compromise — the point.

It means you can leave one running on a screen in the corner of the room, or send it to a board member, or hand a tablet to a volunteer, without any of the anxiety that usually comes with handing someone a live system. The worst thing anybody can do to a dashboard is look at it.

## Everyone sees their own version

Each tile shows *you* only what you're already allowed to see.

So two people can open the same dashboard and correctly see different numbers, and neither of them is looking at a mistake. The access rules you set on projects and documents follow through into the reporting, which means a dashboard is never the accidental back door to information somebody shouldn't have.

## Building one

From an initiative's sidebar: **Dashboards → New dashboard**. Add tiles, point each at what it should read, arrange them on the canvas.

Or skip the building. The [marketplace](apps-and-marketplace.md) has ready-made dashboards you can add in a couple of clicks and then adjust — usually faster than starting from an empty canvas, and often better, because somebody has already found out which four numbers actually matter.

!!! tip "Steal a layout, then change it"
    A marketplace dashboard becomes an ordinary dashboard of yours the moment you add it. Rename it, rearrange it, delete the tiles you don't want. Nothing phones home.

### Widgets from the marketplace

Some marketplace tiles are custom **widgets**. They run in an isolated sandbox and can only hand back something to draw — so a widget that misbehaves shows an error in its own tile while the rest of the page carries on as though nothing happened.

## Where they show up

Dashboards live inside an initiative, next to its projects and documents, and share the same way — **Viewer**, **Editor**, **Owner**, or the whole initiative. Since "look at this" is the entire use case, they're often the one thing a group opens right up.

Each dashboard has its own comment thread, which is a decent place to argue about what a number means. Switch it off under **Settings → Advanced** if you'd rather not.
